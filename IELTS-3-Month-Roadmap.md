# IELTS 3-Month Roadmap: Target Band 7+

A structured 12-week plan for all four IELTS skills: Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking. It is designed for **IELTS Academic** and assumes about **2 hours of focused study a day, 6 days a week**. If you are taking General Training, use GT reading practice and replace Academic Writing Task 1 with letter writing; the other tracks are the same.

## Before you start

1. Confirm whether you will take **Academic** or **General Training**, and choose a test date near the end of Week 12.
2. Take one timed, official-style Listening and Reading test, write both Writing tasks in one sitting, and record a Speaking answer. Use this as your baseline—not as a prediction of your final score.
3. Keep an error log. For every mistake, record the question type, what led you to the wrong answer, and what you will do differently next time.
4. Use the [official IELTS preparation resources](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources) to find current assessment guidance and understand what examiners assess. A Band 7 target depends on performance across the assessed criteria; advanced vocabulary alone is not enough.

## How to use this plan

Plan for **8–10 focused hours a week**, spread over six days; keep one day off. Aim for sessions of about 60–90 minutes. That time includes attempting questions, marking them, and correcting mistakes. If you finish the listed questions early, use the remaining time to review or redo errors rather than piling on extra tests. Do not count background English videos as a full practice session.

Each week below gives you four assignments and a checkable result. Spread them across your six study days; you do not need to study all four skills every day. If a task takes longer, finish the review before adding more questions. Use official sample tests for timed practice, and save some unused questions for the later mock exams.

If you are taking **General Training**, use GT reading passages and do the letter-writing assignment in place of Academic Task 1. Use Week 1 as your baseline, Week 6 as your midpoint check, Week 9 to test your full practice routine, and Week 11 as your final mock. Three months is a target, not a guarantee: if your baseline is far below the level you need, consider moving the test date rather than rushing through the later weeks.

## 12-week plan

### Week 1 — Find your starting point

- **Listening:** Take one complete timed practice test. Mark it, then replay each missed answer and note the section and cause (missed paraphrase, distractor, spelling, or lost concentration).
- **Reading:** Take one complete 60-minute practice test. For every wrong or guessed answer, underline the sentence that proves the correct answer and label the error.
- **Writing:** In one 60-minute sitting, write one Task 1 and one Task 2. Keep both drafts; do not rewrite them yet. Note which task you ran out of time on.
- **Speaking:** Record one complete 11–14 minute mock (Parts 1, 2, and 3). Listen back once and note three specific issues, such as long pauses, repeated grammar errors, or answers that do not develop.
- **End-of-week result:** Save raw Listening/Reading scores, both writing drafts, and your recording. These are your baseline, not a promised band estimate.

### Week 2 — Learn to get answers accurately

- **Listening:** Do two 20–25 minute practice sets: one form/note completion and one multiple-choice set. Before playing audio, predict the answer type; afterward, check spelling, plurals, and word limits.
- **Reading:** Do two timed passages (20 minutes each). Practise skimming each passage for its main point, then scanning for names, dates, and paraphrased keywords.
- **Writing:** Write one Task 2 plan (5 minutes) and one full essay (40 minutes). Separately, spend 30 minutes correcting ten sentences from your own Week 1 writing, focusing on errors you actually made.
- **Speaking:** Record answers to ten Part 1 questions. Keep each answer to roughly 2–4 sentences; replay and redo the three answers that sound least clear.
- **End-of-week result:** Add your five most frequent errors to the error log and make one personal checklist to use before submitting answers.

### Week 3 — Handle common question types

- **Listening:** Complete one matching set and one multiple-choice set. For each wrong answer, identify the phrase in the recording that gives the answer and the distractor that misled you.
- **Reading:** Do one True/False/Not Given set and one matching-headings set. For every answer, write a short evidence note; do not use outside knowledge to decide.
- **Writing:** Write one timed Task 1 (20 minutes) and one Task 2 body paragraph. For Academic Task 1, include an overview and at least two useful comparisons; for GT, cover all three letter bullet points with an appropriate tone.
- **Speaking:** Do three Part 2 cue cards. Take exactly one minute to make brief notes, speak for up to two minutes, then listen back and check whether you addressed every part of the cue card.
- **End-of-week result:** Keep the best Part 2 recording and one corrected Task 1 response.

### Week 4 — Build complete, organized answers

- **Listening:** Complete two timed sections on separate days. After marking, replay only the difficult parts and write down the signal words or paraphrases you missed.
- **Reading:** Complete two passages in 20 minutes each. Spend at least as long reviewing as answering; classify each error as timing, locating evidence, paraphrase, or misunderstanding the question.
- **Writing:** Write one Task 1 in 20 minutes and one Task 2 in 40 minutes on separate days. For Task 2, check that your introduction answers the prompt and each body paragraph has one main idea with support.
- **Speaking:** Practise Part 3 with ten questions across two topics. Give an opinion, explain why, and add an example or consequence. Record at least five answers.
- **End-of-week result:** Review your error log and select the two weakest question types for extra practice in Weeks 5–6.

### Week 5 — Improve paraphrase recognition and idea support

- **Listening:** Do one 30-minute mixed practice section. Before listening, underline key words in the questions; afterward, list five paraphrases used in the audio.
- **Reading:** Do one timed passage with sentence completion and one with matching information. For each answer, identify the matching wording in the passage.
- **Writing:** Write one Task 2 essay in 40 minutes. Underline the sentence that states your position and the evidence/example supporting each main point; revise any paragraph without both.
- **Speaking:** Record one full mock and two extra Part 2 cue cards. On playback, note unclear words and repeated fillers, then repeat only the weakest answers more clearly.
- **End-of-week result:** Create a short list of ten useful words or phrases you encountered in context and write your own sentence for each; do not memorize isolated word lists.

### Week 6 — Midpoint check

- **Listening:** Take a new complete timed practice test. Compare the score and error types with Week 1; review every wrong answer and every correct guess.
- **Reading:** Take a new complete 60-minute test. Record the score by passage/question type and check whether you finished within time.
- **Writing:** Complete Task 1 and Task 2 together in 60 minutes. Self-check task coverage, organization, vocabulary, and grammar using the checklist below; revise one task after a break.
- **Speaking:** Do one full mock, recorded in one sitting. Compare it with Week 1 for fewer pauses, clearer organization, and more complete answers.
- **End-of-week result:** Write a one-page progress note: what improved, what remains weak, and the two priorities for the next three weeks. If scores are not improving, reduce new-question volume and spend more time reviewing mistakes.

### Week 7 — Repair your two weakest areas

- **Listening:** Use two 25-minute sets for your weakest question type. Pause only after the first attempt is marked; then replay and explain the answer signal in your own words.
- **Reading:** Complete two passages focused on your weakest types. Before checking the key, point to the exact evidence for each answer, especially Not Given answers.
- **Writing:** Rewrite one Week 1 or Week 6 response from scratch using the same prompt. Compare task coverage, paragraph order, and repeated grammar errors with the original.
- **Speaking:** Record six Part 3 answers on two topics. For each answer, use the sequence “answer → reason → example”; redo any answer that is underdeveloped or off-topic.
- **End-of-week result:** Update your error log. Mark errors that have disappeared and keep the ones that recur.

### Week 8 — Practise under stricter timing

- **Listening:** Complete one full test under test conditions. Do not pause or replay; review it only after finishing.
- **Reading:** Complete one full 60-minute test. Use a simple time plan (about 20 minutes per passage); if one question stalls, mark it and move on.
- **Writing:** Do one Task 2 in 40 minutes, including a five-minute plan and final check. On another day, write Task 1 in 20 minutes.
- **Speaking:** Record one full mock and listen back at normal speed. Note whether each answer directly addresses the question and whether your pace is understandable.
- **End-of-week result:** Compare your test scores and timing with Week 6. Do not treat a single practice score as a guaranteed exam band.

### Week 9 — Full exam practice and detailed review

- **Listening:** Take a new complete timed test. Check every answer and replay missed sections; write one sentence about the cause of each repeated error.
- **Reading:** Take a new complete 60-minute test. Mark guessed answers separately from confident ones, then check evidence for both groups.
- **Writing:** Complete both tasks in one 60-minute sitting. Spend the final five minutes checking the prompt, paragraph purpose, verb forms, articles, and spelling.
- **Speaking:** Complete a full mock with fresh questions. If possible, ask a partner to give you unseen questions; otherwise use a prompt list and do not prepare scripted answers.
- **End-of-week result:** Pick only two remaining weaknesses to repair before the final mock; avoid trying to change every habit at once.

### Week 10 — Targeted correction, not more volume

- **Listening:** Do two 20-minute sets chosen from your error log. After each, practise the exact missed skill (for example, following a speaker change or noticing a correction).
- **Reading:** Do two passages targeting your weakest types. Review wrong answers by finding the evidence and explaining why the tempting option is wrong.
- **Writing:** Write one Task 1 and one Task 2 on separate days. If a teacher or capable English speaker can review them, ask for comments on task coverage and clarity; otherwise use the official criteria and rewrite your weaker response.
- **Speaking:** Record two short mocks on different days. Compare them with Week 9 and work on just one delivery issue and one answer-development issue.
- **End-of-week result:** Make a one-page exam checklist from recurring errors; bring it to Week 11 practice.

### Week 11 — Final full mock

- **Listening, Reading, and Writing:** Complete all three papers in order in one uninterrupted sitting (about 2 hours 40 minutes). Follow the test instructions and use your checklist, but do not add new techniques or memorized phrases.
- **Speaking:** Do one final full mock with unseen prompts, on the same day or a separate day. Keep it conversational; listen once and note only the two most useful final corrections.
- **End-of-week result:** Review scores, timing, and task coverage. Decide whether you are ready to sit the planned test; if your results are still well below your required level, consider rescheduling if possible.

### Week 12 — Light review and recovery

- **Listening:** Early in the week, do one 20-minute set, then review your error log. Stop full tests.
- **Reading:** Early in the week, do one passage with a comfortable time limit. Review strategy, not unfamiliar vocabulary lists.
- **Writing:** Early in the week, plan one Task 2 and write one Task 1 outline. Do not write multiple full essays in the final days.
- **Speaking:** Do two relaxed 10-minute practices using fresh questions. Prioritize clear answers and rest; do not rehearse scripts.
- **End-of-week result:** Confirm test-day logistics, identification, travel/arrival time, and the test-specific instructions. Keep the final day before the exam free from heavy study.

## Track checklists

### Listening

- Read each question before its audio section starts; predict the kind of answer needed.
- Watch for paraphrases, corrections, distractors, singular/plural forms, spelling, and word limits.
- After marking, replay difficult parts and identify exactly where the answer was signalled.
- Track performance by section and question type; practise the weakest area instead of repeating only comfortable questions.

### Reading

- Follow the instructions and word limits exactly.
- Locate the relevant text, then compare its meaning with the question; do not answer from outside knowledge.
- Treat **Not Given** as “the passage does not say,” not “probably false.”
- Practise pacing and leave time to check unanswered or uncertain questions.

### Writing

- **Task 1 Academic:** select the main trends/features, write a clear overview, and compare relevant data. Do not describe every number.
- **Task 1 General Training:** make the purpose clear, address every bullet point, and keep the tone appropriate.
- **Task 2:** answer every part of the prompt, take a clear position where needed, and support main ideas with explanations or examples.
- Leave a few minutes to check task coverage, paragraphing, grammar, and spelling. Do not force complex vocabulary or memorised templates.
- Task 2 carries more weight than Task 1, so give it the larger share of your Writing practice.

### Speaking

- Answer the question first, then develop the answer with a reason, detail, or example.
- In Part 2, use brief notes and a simple sequence of events or ideas; do not write a full script.
- In Part 3, explain and support your ideas rather than giving only a yes/no opinion.
- Record yourself regularly. Review clarity, pace, grammar patterns, and pronunciation—not whether you sound like a native speaker.

## Checkpoints and adjustment rules

Review these at the end of Weeks 4, 6, 8, and 11:

1. Compare timed Listening and Reading results with Week 1, including whether you finished on time and which question types still cost marks. Use a score conversion only when it is supplied for that practice test.
2. Check whether Writing answers fully address the prompts and whether Speaking answers are developed, clear, and understandable. Self-assessment is useful but cannot reliably predict an official Writing or Speaking band.
3. Choose no more than **two** recurring weaknesses for the next block of study. Keep a small amount of practice in the other skills so they do not go cold.
4. If Writing or Speaking remains difficult and you can access a teacher or capable reviewer, use their feedback on one response before changing your approach. Free automated writing feedback is not an IELTS band score.

## On test day

- Check your test venue or online-test instructions, identification requirements, and arrival time in advance.
- Use the question instructions and transfer/check answers as permitted by your test format.
- If one question feels difficult, make your best choice and continue; protect time for the remaining questions.
- The final week is for consolidation and rest—not an exhausting increase in study hours.
